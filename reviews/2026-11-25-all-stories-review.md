## Main problems across the set
1. **The same origin story is told three times.** The first client, the uncle mentor, and the EUR 100-120 / EUR 280 projects appear in stories 3, 6 and 7 (and parts in 5). Readers who open two of them will see repeats. Keep the full version in story 6 and link to it from the others with one sentence.
2. **Two different audiences.** Stories 1 and 2 attract working developers using AI agents. Stories 4 to 7 attract students and juniors. Both are valid, but the profile has no signal for which is primary. Use lists ("AI Coding Workflows", "Career Journey") and pin story 1.
3. **Story 7 title mismatch** (it covers 2020 to 2026, not "first year"). Story 5 overlaps story 7 on the same job timeline (Alphabase, MicroMerger, promotion).
4. **Story 1 is long (14 min) for a first-read.** Consider splitting into a series: Memory files, Skills, Quality gates, with a series hub.
5. **Claims needing sources or links:** news coverage (UrduPoint, ARY, PNN: link from GitHub README), and any technical claims in stories 1 and 2 (Supabase RLS, PGRST116, Claude Code and Antigravity skill locations) should link to official docs.
6. **Style habits:** many one-line paragraphs, recap lists at the end, and "Press enter or click to view image" images with generated thumbnails. Story 1 notes an AI-generated thumbnail, which is honest; keep disclosing.
7. **Medium page setup:** empty About, no photo confirmed, 2 followers, no publication submissions.
8. **Tags:** story 4 uses 4 AI tags that are near duplicates ("Ai Replacing Jobs", "Ai Replacing Human", "Ai Replace Developers"). Use Programming, Software Engineering, Career Advice, AI.
